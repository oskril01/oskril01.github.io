Welcome

I want to understand how prior scientific knowledge shapes machine learning architecture choices. My approach uses geometric, statistical, and algebraic tools to probe the latent representations of models. By studying how constraints influence both the training process and the resulting latent spaces, I aim to develop systematic methods for designing intelligent systems that fully leverage existing scientific knowledge. I am particularly interested in the application of physics informed machine learning for chemical and biological problems, and the application of tools from statistical physics to data science.

I studied chemical engineering, mathematics, and chemical physics as an undergraduate, and I’m currently pursuing a master’s degree in mathematics with a concentration in the foundations of machine learning at Tufts University. This site collects my research, projects, and artwork.

Outside of academics, I’m an avid reader and artist. I love to draw, paint, and spend time traveling and in museums. I also enjoy skiing, running, hiking, and doing flips.
